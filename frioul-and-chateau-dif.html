Frioul & Château d'If: the islands that stay open when everything closes

Four islands in the middle of the national park, served by a 35-minute public ferry that runs every day of the year — and, thanks to a legal quirk no English guide mentions, the one place in the Calanques you can still legally walk, swim and snorkel on a red fire-risk day. Also: the fortress Dumas made famous, its very real luggage trap, and ferry fares that jumped 60% this year.

TL;DR

  • Frioul does not close with the massif. On a red fire-risk day, when every mainland trail in the park is illegal, the park's own rules say "la visite de l'archipel du Frioul reste possible" — the ferry runs, the trails are open, the snorkel trail is open. If a closure just killed your calanque plan, this is the plan B.
  • 2026 fares rose ~60%: €17.70 one-island return, €26 for both islands, €9.60 single, and a €5.60 evening return July–September. Every guide quoting ~€11 is stale.
  • Château d'If has traps: no lockers and suitcases refused at the door, swimming banned, smoking banned site-wide, and a landing pier that is the only non-wheelchair-accessible one on the route. The château also closes in bad weather on a decision made separately from whether the ferry sails.
  • The Saint-Estève snorkel trail is free — one of only two in the park — but the beach carried a 2026 water-quality advisory and gets none of the toilets or lockers other lifeguarded beaches do. Both facts below.

The red-day loophole (the headline nobody prints)

From 1 June to 30 September the préfecture rates the Bouches-du-Rhône forest massifs daily for fire risk, and a red rating closes a massif completely — all land access banned, including arriving by sea. The Frioul islands are not a rated massif. They carry no forest to burn, they sit outside the fire-decree's scope, and the National Park says it in one plain sentence in its own regulations: on a journée rouge, while land access to the park is forbidden and all landing from boats is prohibited, "la visite de l'archipel du Frioul reste possible" — visiting the Frioul archipelago remains possible.

Red day — mainland

Everything on land, banned

All trails, all calanques, all crags, from Sugiton to En-Vau — even the coastal path at Callelongue, 6 km across the water. Sugiton permits are cancelled by email, not transferred. Fines start at €135 per person. The full mechanics are on the closures page.

Red day — Frioul

Open: walk, swim, snorkel

The ferry runs, the 9 km trail network is open, the beaches are open, and the free Saint-Estève snorkel trail is open — all of it legally, inside the national park core. On a red day you can swim on Frioul while being unable to set foot on the shore you can see from your towel.

Open is not exempt. The core-zone fire rules still apply on the islands, red day or not: no fire, no barbecue, no camp stove, no smoking. The vegetation is low, dry and highly flammable, and there is no fire service out there.

The self-guided version of this plan B is the public ferry below. The packaged version — for people who want commentary, a skipper and a swim stop off the boat folded into one booking — is the Frioul + Château d'If boat-and-swim combo (★4.6, 613 reviews) — it was booking about 14 times a day when we checked, which on a closure day tells you where everyone else went. One caveat that applies to every red-day plan: anyone arriving on their own boat should confirm the day's landing rules with the maritime prefecture rather than reasoning from a webpage.

Ferries & the 2026 fares Verified: 30 July 2026

There is exactly one realistic way in: the RTM public ferry lebateau Frioul If from the Gare Maritime at 1 quai de la Fraternité — the head of the Vieux-Port, 10–15 minutes from Saint-Charles station on metro M1 (see getting there). It runs 7 days a week, all year, from about 06:30, on three 160-passenger vessels built for this specific crossing. About 20 minutes to the île d'If, about 35 minutes to the Port du Frioul. Daytime sailings normally call at If then continue to Frioul; summer evening sailings run direct to Frioul and skip If entirely.

The stale-price trap: fares rose about 60% for 2026 — the one-island return went from €11.10 to €17.70. Almost every English-language source still quotes ~€11. The figures below are the operator's own, in force from 1 July 2026.

The evening window, and what it's worth

From 1 July to 30 September, departures from the Vieux-Port at 19:20, 20:30, 21:40 and 22:45 (plus an 18:10 boat outside the cheap fare) return from Frioul as late as 23:10, with a final arrival back at the Vieux-Port at 00:45 — and any departure from 19:00 costs €5.60 return. Sunset over the Côte Bleue from Pomègues is the best free thing the archipelago offers. The paid upgrade on the same idea is the evening sailing trip to the Frioul calanques with buffet and wine (★4.8, 149 reviews, top-rated) — that one is for people who want the seaward coves a walker can't reach, dinner handled, and no interest in catching the 23:10 ferry home. More options on the boat tours page and under sailing & catamaran.

The If-only alternative

A private operator, Icard Maritime ("Calanques If"), serves the château only, 8 February–11 November: €14 flat for a ~50-minute bay cruise plus at least an hour on If, with the €7 monument entry sold together at its Vieux-Port office. Same-day counter booking. Note its own restrictions: no passengers 7+ months pregnant; manual wheelchairs yes, electric by prior arrangement — though the If pier problem below applies regardless of the boat.

From Cassis there is no boat at all — it's train to Marseille, metro, then ferry, realistically 2h15–2h45 each way. If you're based in Cassis, the Cassis-side calanques are the better day — unless the massif is closed, in which case Frioul is the best legal option in the entire park.

Crowds, and the quiet windows

The archipelago takes over half a million visitors a year, essentially all through one pier on one boat — so boats and beaches fill together. The 10:00–14:00 sailings are the crush; police patrols ride the shuttles on peak days. The windows that work:

  • The first boat of the day — raking light on the Marseille skyline, an empty Saint-Estève, and the walking done before the heat.
  • Anything after 18:00 in summer, helped by the €5.60 fare and last returns near midnight.
  • The Pomègues side, any time — most day-trippers never cross the digue Berry.
  • May–June and September–early October — the sea is warmest in September and the snorkel trail is still in. Winter is genuinely empty and the light on the white limestone is at its best; remember the château closes Mondays October–March.
  • Any red fire-risk day — the loophole above. Most visitors don't know it.

Which version of the day is yours

Frioul is the strongest do-it-yourself case in the park after Port-Miou: the transport is public, cheap, accessible, year-round, and lands exactly where a tour would. Route yourself honestly:

Château d'If: Monte-Cristo's prison, and its visit-condition traps

François I ordered the fortress after visiting the island in 1516; construction began in 1524, making it the oldest known fortification in the archipelago. For 400 years it held real prisoners — Protestants after the revocation of the Edict of Nantes, political detainees, the quarantined sick — whose graffiti still mark the walls. The prisoner everyone crosses for, Edmond Dantès, never existed; Dumas's fiction simply outgrew the building. Without that context it's a courtyard, spiral stairs and a superb view back at Marseille; the Centre des monuments nationaux's commented and family visits (including a storytelling walk from age 6 on selected Sundays) are the one genuinely additive paid product on the islands, and worth it for anyone here for the book.

Admission is €7, paid at the monument — it is not included in the RTM ferry ticket, a constant source of confusion. Free: under-18s, 18–25-year-old EU nationals or lawful residents in France, disabled visitors plus a companion, jobseekers, and the first Sunday of the month November–March. Pre-booking via the CMN ticketing site (tickets.monuments-nationaux.fr — plain text on purpose) is sensible on busy summer days. Download e-tickets before you sail: the signal at the monument entrance is poor.

Hours Verified: 30 July 2026: 10:00–18:00 April–September; 10:00–17:00 October–March, closed Mondays in that period, and closed 1 January, 1 May and 25 December. The last boat back from If in summer 2026 is 17:55 — the monument closes at 18:00 with no late opening.

The four traps, in descending order of ruined days

  • No lockers, luggage refused. There are no lockers anywhere on the island, and suitcases and large rucksacks are turned away at the door. Planning If between a train and a hotel check-in is the classic way to pay for a crossing you can't use. Stash bags in Marseille first.
  • The If pier is the only non-wheelchair-accessible one. Boats and every other landing stage on the route are accessible; If's is not, and the monument is steps and spiral stairs throughout. Mobility-limited visitors should buy the one-island Frioul ticket, not the two-island.
  • Bad weather closes the château on a separate decision from the ferry. Southerly or south-easterly swell breaking on the exposed If landing stage shuts the monument while the boat still sails to Frioul. In doubtful weather, buying the €26 two-island ticket can buy you a €17.70 outcome.
  • Swimming from the island is banned. Smoking is banned site-wide, inside and out, under décret n° 2025-582 of 27 June 2025. Also refused: knives, aerosols, alcohol, and any dog that isn't an assistance dog.

Time budget: If alone is 3–3h30 door to door from the Vieux-Port. Both islands plus the château is a genuine 7–9-hour day — take the first boat, do If while you're fresh and the courtyard is quiet, then Frioul for the afternoon and a swim. Aim for the first or last sailing either way: mid-morning to early afternoon compresses every ferry-load into the same courtyard, staircase and terrace at once.

One calendar note: the Défi de Monte-Cristo open-water swim — from the château back to the Prado, 28th edition — put thousands of swimmers in the If channel on 5–7 and 13–14 June 2026 (with a La Ciotat edition 11–13 September). If your visit lands on a future edition's weekend, expect a very different bay.

Saint-Estève & the free snorkel trail — the good and the flagged

The sentier sous-marin at Saint-Estève is one of only two free, self-guided, marked snorkel trails in the entire national park (the other is Plage des Catalans in Marseille). Five numbered buoys carry information panels immersed just below the surface, all in under 5 m of water, inside a ZRUB — a bathing-reserved zone from which vessels are totally excluded, so nothing motorised comes anywhere near the swimmers. It's in place roughly July to September, costs nothing, and nothing is lent or hired — bring your own mask and snorkel. Minors must be accompanied by an adult. The park lists dusky grouper, seahorse, red coral and the fan mussel in Frioul waters; at trail depth expect the small rocky-bottom and posidonia species, with grouper a possibility rather than a promise.

The two negatives the brochures skip

  • Water quality: Saint-Estève appeared on the 2026 "La Belle Plage" ranking — built from the health agency's own sampling data — among the Marseille beaches where bathing is advised against. That is an advisory, not an administrative ban: check the daily flag at the beach and the city's météo des plages before you swim.
  • No toilets, no lockers: the city's 2026 bathing-season announcement provides free toilets and lockers at all supervised beach posts except Borély, Sormiou and Saint-Estève — though the tourist office listing still shows a toilets icon. Treat facilities outside the port village as unreliable.

Saint-Estève is the archipelago's only lifeguarded beach — the 2026 city bathing season runs 29 May–30 August, supervised zones watched 09:30–18:45 city-wide — and it is also the most crowded: the park itself calls it surfréquentée in summer. It fills by late morning in July–August, and the "beach" everywhere on Frioul is coarse sand, gravel and rounded stone — water shoes are a comfort item you will actually use. The alternatives:

Morgiret — the near one

Under 10 minutes from the pier, no steps, stroller-feasible. Stonier than Saint-Estève, with big rounded stones at the waterline and two small side coves. Poorly protected from the mistral. Full beach rundown on the swimming & snorkeling page.

La Crine ("plage de Tahiti") — the local pick

On Pomègues, ~30 minutes' walk across the digue. Sheltered from wind and swell, translucent water, and the beach locals name first — which is also why August punishes it. Pair it with the two-island walk.

The seaward coves — boat only

No path serves the outer flanks of Pomègues; a sailing or catamaran trip is the only way to swim there without your own boat. The catamaran to Frioul with swim stop and snacks is currently GetYourGuide's #1-selling guided tour for Marseille — on a still-light review base (★4.5, 28 reviews). Both facts, honestly.

Beyond the named three there's a string of tiny inlets — l'Eoube, the Grand Soufre, the coves around Cap de Croix — that reward the full two-island walk. Divers: Frioul is a serious destination in its own right, with named sites from the shallow immersed-amphora archaeological plot under the Pomègues semaphore (open to most certified divers) to the Junker 88 bomber lost in 1943, lying at about 53 m — a level-3 dive, not a casual one. All of it is boat-access and centre-led, and seven of the archipelago's calanques are closed to scuba outright (next section). Browse dive & snorkel trips for the guided options. One naming trap for dive planning: Tiboulen du Frioul is not Tiboulen de Maïre — two different rocks, two different archipelagos, and booking the wrong one wastes a day.

Rules on the islands (the ones visitors actually break)

Frioul sits in the national park core, plus two instruments of its own: a municipal arrêté of 5 February 2021 covering the land, and the Mediterranean maritime prefect's ZIEM decree covering the water. The short version:

RuleDetail
Bikes banned outrightThe 5 Feb 2021 arrêté bans cycling on all natural land areas — only the village zone and the hôpital Caroline road are exempt. E-bikes over 250 W count as motor vehicles and are excluded everywhere in the core. No operator can lawfully run a Frioul bike or e-bike tour — any such product is selling an illegal activity, and the terrain is flat enough that you don't miss the bike.
Climbing bannedSame 2021 arrêté. The sea cliffs look inviting; they're off-limits, full stop.
7 calanques are ZIEMUnder the maritime prefect's decree (n°238 of 28 June 2024, reissued annually), motorised craft may neither navigate nor anchor in seven Frioul calanques — and scuba and spearfishing are prohibited in them. An apnea (breath-hold) carve-out applies at Saint-Estève. A dive centre offering you a closed calanque is not authorised, whatever it says.
ZRUB = no vesselsThe Saint-Estève bathing zone excludes all craft — do not paddle a kayak or SUP through it.
Dogs: leashed yes, If noDogs on a lead are allowed on Frioul and on the ferry — a rarity among French national parks. Only assistance dogs enter the château; dogs are banned entirely on the Riou archipelago. Bring water: the limestone gets ferociously hot.
No fire, no camping, no binsFire, barbecue or stove: €135 per person. Bivouac or camping: €68 per person, all year. Bins were deliberately removed park-wide — carry everything out, fruit peel included. No amplified sound. Don't pick anything, especially the protected sea daffodil, and stay on waymarked paths — the island is a major seabird nesting site.
Drones & fishingMotorised overflight of the core needs prior authorisation from the park director — treat recreational drone flying as prohibited without a permit. Recreational fishing anywhere in the park requires a free annual declaration plus per-trip catch declarations, with no-take zones and seasonal species rules on top.
Ruins are unsecuredThe forts, bunkers and the hôpital Caroline are walk-around, not walk-in. Keep children off the unfenced fortification edges.

The walking, and the new Maison des îles

Everything starts at the ferry quay — there is no other trailhead. The park's flagship route, the Tour de l'île de Ratonneau, is graded EASY: 3.8 km, 1h15, 110 m of ascent, looping past the Saint-Estève calanque, around (not into) the hôpital Caroline — the neoclassical quarantine hospital opened in 1828 — and up to the fort de Ratonneau, where strange concrete crosses survive from a wartime decoy built to make an ammunition dump read as a cemetery from the air. The full two-island circuit over the digue Berry onto wilder, emptier Pomègues is about 9 km and 200 m of ascent — 3–5 hours with a swim. No scrambling anywhere; the limiting factor is heat, glare and the total absence of shade and water. Centuries of deforestation and garrisons left pines the height of your ankle. Two litres per person minimum in summer, a hat, and trainers not sandals. Guided-walk alternatives on the mainland are covered under guided hikes and the hiking page.

Maison des îles et du littoral — new, free, and worth routing past

A restored military villa 15 minutes' walk from the port, co-run by the City of Marseille and the National Park — open since June 2025 and formally inaugurated 21 May 2026. Free entry. 2026 hours: March, April, June, September, October, November — Wednesday–Sunday 09:30–16:00; July–August — Tuesday–Sunday 11:00–17:00. No hours are published for May or December–February — assume closed then and check before relying on it. The outdoor interpretation panels on landscapes, marine life and the bay's history can be read even when the building is shut. Verified: 30 July 2026

Two mobility-friendly assists: the petit train du Frioul runs a ~30-minute circuit from opposite the landing stage to the hôpital Caroline via Saint-Estève, 1 July–1 September only, €4 adult / €2 child return, timed to ferry arrivals. And the port village itself — the archipelago's only serviced area — is flat, with restaurants (roughly €20–35 a head), small shops and the only drinking water on the islands. Worked example for budgeting: a couple doing both islands and the château pays €66 before food; the same couple doing Frioul on the evening fare with a picnic pays €11.20.

Overnight? Realistically no — bivouac and camping are banned throughout the core all year, and the island's one accommodation centre takes school and diving groups only, not individual bookings. The €5.60 evening ticket is the legitimate way to have the island at dusk.

What to bring (and what to leave)

  • Bring: 2 L of water per person minimum (more for both islands), hat, high-factor sunscreen, sunglasses — the white limestone doubles the glare. Trainers, not sandals. Reef or water shoes for every beach, without exception. Mask and snorkel July–September. A windproof layer for the crossing, even in August. A picnic and your own bin bag. A leash if a dog comes.
  • Leave behind: the bike (banned), climbing gear (banned), the drone (authorisation required), any stove or barbecue (banned) — and the suitcase, if the château d'If is on the itinerary (refused at the door, no lockers).

Wind logic: the crossing runs into the mistral — and sails anyway

Do not transfer Cassis logic to Marseille. Cassis sits in the mistral's lee and loses boats to easterly and southerly swell; the Frioul crossing is the opposite case — it runs across the open rade straight into the mistral. But the city states the shuttles were purpose-built to make this crossing "même par fort mistral", and they run all year. In practice a mistral day means a rough, spray-lashed, cold 35 minutes — take a windproof layer even in August — not a cancellation.

What actually stops your plans, in order: heavy seas suspend sailings (subscribe to the operator's infos trafic alerts for the day of travel); and — more often — the château d'If closes in bad weather while the ferry sails on to Frioul, because the two decisions are made separately. If the swell is southerly or south-easterly, breaking on the exposed If pier, assume the château is the casualty and buy the one-island ticket.

On the islands the wind is a comfort issue, not a safety one — but it picks your beach for you. Mistral: Saint-Estève, facing east towards Marseille, is sheltered (and a mistral day is often the better snorkelling day there); Morgiret is not. Easterly: reverse it — head across to the Pomègues side, and write off the snorkel trail, which loses its visibility and pitches its buoys. Paddlers: any crossing from the mainland is a mistral question first — an offshore mistral pushes a kayak away from the coast, and this is open, ferry-trafficked water; see kayak & SUP before committing.
