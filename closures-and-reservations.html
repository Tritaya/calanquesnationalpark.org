Closures & reservations: the three-layer system, decoded

Three independent mechanisms decide whether you can stand where you plan to stand tomorrow — a nightly fire map, municipal road calendars, and one calanque's permit system. Most travel content mashes them into a single wrong rule. Here is each layer, from the actual French legal texts.

TL;DR

  • Every evening, June–September: tomorrow's access is published per massif around 17:00–18:00. The outcome is binary — open or red. Red means no land access at all, even from the sea.
  • Road closures are a separate, calendar-based layer that only affects cars — and it runs even on days the massif itself is open.
  • Exactly one calanque needs a permit: Sugiton, free, booked 3 days to 1 day ahead. En-Vau needs nothing. If you read only one page before a summer visit, make it this one.

Layer 1 — the nightly fire map (how to actually check it)

From 1 June to 30 September the préfecture rates every massif in the Bouches-du-Rhône for tomorrow's fire danger and converts that into an access decision. The official channel named in the arêté is:

risque-prevention-incendie.fr/13

Open it after ~18:00, switch the language toggle to English (it works but doesn't stick between pages), select the Tomorrow tab, and find your massif: Calanques for everything Marseille-side, Cap Canaille for the Cassis–La Ciotat cliffs, Saint-Cyr for the far east. We measured the 2026 season: median publication 17:00, 95% published by 18:00, zero same-day revisions.

The decision is binary. Four internal risk levels collapse into two outcomes: accessible, or red (closed). Sites explaining that "orange means you can still walk carefully" are quoting the 2018 arêté that was expressly abrogated in 2025 — the current texts are the April 2025 base order and its July 2026 revision, and neither has an "open-ish" state.

Two dead channels to ignore: the MyProvence Balade app named in older official communications no longer exists in either app store, and the 0811 phone line has been dropped from the 2026 arêté (and never worked from foreign SIMs anyway). The park's own Mes Calanques app is live but French-first and covers only the Calanques massif. The website above is the channel that works.

Reality check for planners: red days are rarer than the drama suggests — the coastal Calanques massif has gone red 3 times so far in 2026, versus 8 department-wide. Inland massifs burn; the coast mostly doesn't. But when it's red, it's genuinely, enforcedly closed.

Layer 1½ — what red actually forbids (and what it doesn't)

Banned on red

Everything on land

All trails, all calanques, all crags — the ban covers arrival "y compris par la mer" (including by sea). Kayakers may paddle and moor but not set foot on shore. Callelongue's coastal path and Cassis's Port-Miou path close with the massif — two spots travel content wrongly lists as always-open. Guides and outdoor professionals get zero exemption.

Still legal on red

The water, and the edges

Boat tours sail normally — navigation was never the banned part (and no boat may land here on any day). Built-up edges stay open: Callelongue's hamlet, Les Goudes, Cassis town beaches. And the Frioul archipelago is not a rated massif — the ferry runs, the snorkel trail is open, Château d'If receives visitors.

One asymmetry worth memorising: a booking never opens a closed massif, but a closed massif cancels your booking. Sugiton permits for a red day are voided by email the evening before — not deferred, not transferred.

Layer 2 — the road gates (cars only, calendar-based)

Restaurant reservations historically got vehicle passes, but the schemes vary by calanque and year — ask the restaurant when booking. Better: skip the car entirely (the bus network that works).

Layer 3 — the Sugiton permit, mechanically

One calanque — Sugiton with Pierres-Tombées — nearly died of its own fame: up to 2,500 visitors a day pre-quota. Since 2022, summer access takes a free reserved permit capping the cove at 400 people simultaneously (the figure is confirmed by the state biodiversity agency OFB, though the park's own page doesn't publish it).

2026 dates & mechanics

  • Permit required: 20–21 June · daily 27 June–30 August · weekends 5–6 and 12–13 September. Extended through 2027.
  • Booking window: opens D−3 at 09:00, closes D−1 at 18:00, on the park's Troov portal (linked from the park's Sugiton page — use the French page; both English versions have carried wrong dates this year).
  • Limits: 5 people per booking · 8 bookings per person per season · under-3s exempt but named.
  • At the gate: photo ID for every person, and the permit downloaded or printed — the park's own warning: there's barely any signal at the cove. A permit living in your webmail is unopenable exactly where you need it.
  • Enforcement: an info point, then a staffed barrier where the descent paths start, then roving sworn agents inside the cove — 30–50 people a day were turned back in season one, "notamment des étrangers," facing the 45-minute climb back out.

Sold out? The two windows that work

Troov's own operations data says 30–40% of bookings are never used, and released places re-enter the pool. So: hit the portal at 09:00 sharp three days out, or sweep it late afternoon the day before (before the 18:00 cutoff) when no-shows get recycled. Measured attendance runs 200–250 against the 400 cap — the system has slack; the interface just hides it.

The permit-free version: the reservation zone covers the shoreline only. The Sugiton belvédère (245 m), the col, and the ridge crags are all outside it — no booking, ever, even mid-August. It's the best effort-to-view ratio in the park, 45 minutes from the Luminy bus stop. And through-hikers on the GR traverse never enter the zone at all.

Guided hikes get no bypass — every participant needs a public-quota place. What a guide does buy you is someone who plays the booking windows for the group and re-plans around a red-day cancellation: see the guided hikes and their notes.

The red-day playbook

Tomorrow went red at 17:30 and your one calanques day is cancelled? This is the salvage plan, in order of how much of the original dream each option preserves:

1 · See them anyway — by sea

Navigation is legal on red days; you lose the landing you were never getting anyway. The most-booked swim cruise (★4.7, 1,409 reviews) even keeps the swimming — open water, off the boat.

2 · Use the loophole island

Frioul isn't a rated massif: trails, coves and the Saint-Estève snorkel trail all stay open. The Frioul + Château d'If boat-and-swim combo was booking ~14 times a day when we checked — or just take the public ferry.

3 · Go under the sea, indoors

The Cosquer cave replica — the painted prehistoric cave whose real entrance is 37 m under Cap Morgiou — is air-conditioned, timed-entry, and completely weatherproof. Entry ticket with audio guide.

Layer-2-only day (roads gated, massif open)? Nothing is lost — take the bus like everyone who read this page.
