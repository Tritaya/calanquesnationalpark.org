Marseille · Cassis · La Ciotat — France

The Calanques, without the guesswork

White limestone fjords with Caribbean-blue water, twenty minutes from France's second city — and a rulebook that changes nightly in summer. This is the English guide the park never wrote: who can go where, when, and the honest way to choose between hiking in, paddling in, or seeing it all from a deck.

TL;DR — the three things that decide your visit

  • Summer access is decided nightly. From June to September every massif is declared open or closed for the next day at ~18:00. One calanque (Sugiton) also needs a free reserved permit. Our closures & reservations guide is the five-minute version of the whole system.
  • No boat will land you anywhere. Landing is banned park-wide — boat tours are deck views plus (sometimes) an open-water swim. To stand on a beach you walk in (hiking guide) or paddle in (kayak guide).
  • The park provides nothing — on purpose. No water, no bins, almost no shade, two lifeguarded beaches (Sormiou and Saint-Estève on Frioul). Carry 1.5 L per person per half-day, double in heat, and pack everything out.

The calanques, ranked by what they're actually like

Not a beauty contest — they're all beautiful. Ranked by the gap between what you expect and what you get, with the effort stated honestly. Every entry links to a full guide.

  1. En-Vau — the cathedral

    The postcard: 100-metre cliffs boxing in a wedge of impossible turquoise. The fine print: a steep, polished descent, ~3 h on foot from Marseille's side or 2 h from Cassis, up to 1,200 people at once on a beach the park says fits 400 comfortably. No reservation needed — effort is the filter.

  2. Sormiou — the liveable one

    The biggest calanque, a real (car-restricted) road, cabanons, one restaurant — closed all July–August 2026, which every other guide misses — and one of only two lifeguarded beaches in the park. The family default, if you respect the road-closure calendar.

    Sand + pebbleLifeguards (summer)Road closes daily 12 Jun–30 Aug E-bike tours go here →

  3. Port-Pin — the family one

    45 minutes of moderate trail from Cassis, pine shade behind a small beach, and the official park grade is MOYEN — the only genuinely family-friendly walk-in calanque on the Cassis side. Its neighbour Port-Miou is a 500-boat marina fjord: the easiest calanque of all, but no real beach.

  4. Sugiton — the one with the permit

    So loved it nearly died: 2,500 visitors a day pre-quota. Now summer access takes a free reserved permit (400 people at once), which makes an August afternoon here feel like the 1980s. The belvédère above it needs no permit at all — the best effort-to-view ratio in the park.

    Permit in summerVia LuminyBelvédère free How the permit works →

  5. Morgiou — the fishing hamlet

    A working micro-port pinched between climbing walls, a narrow beach, and the Nautic Bar — now a genuine rarity: a sit-down fresh-fish restaurant inside the park core (Sormiou's famous Le Lunch was demolished in 2017; old guides still list it). The Cosquer cave's real entrance is under Cap Morgiou here, 37 m below the sea.

    Hard-ish hikeTiny beachSnorkel cave nearby Full guide →

  6. Frioul archipelago — the loophole

    Ferries from the Vieux-Port, Monte-Cristo's Château d'If, and calanques of its own with a marked snorkel trail. Crucially: Frioul is not a rated fire massif — it stays open on red days when every mainland trail is shut. 2026 ferry fares jumped ~60% (€17.70 one island); most sites still quote the old price.

  7. Mugel & Figuerolles (La Ciotat) — the other rock

    Ochre puddingstone instead of white limestone, minutes from La Ciotat's centre, and the least-regulated paddling base in the park. Above them: Cap Canaille, the highest sea cliff in metropolitan France, and the Route des Crêtes — car-free every Sunday, all year.

  8. Callelongue & the wild south coast — the end of the world

    The hamlet is literally nicknamed that. Beyond it: Marseilleveyre, Podestat and hours of the emptiest coast in the park — no road, no water (except the Callelongue fountain, the last tap going east), and the stretch the park itself names for unpredictable currents. Experienced walkers only, rewarded accordingly.

Boat, kayak, or your own two feet — the honest comparison

The single most-asked question, and the answer depends on one fact most visitors learn too late: no commercial boat may land anywhere in the park. That splits the options cleanly.

Boat tour — see the most, touch the least

8–13 calanques in a half-day, zero sweat. From Cassis: commentary circuits (€21–33, no stops) or one €40 swim-stop trip; from Marseille: swim-stop cruises and catamaran days. You will not stand on a beach.

Boat tours →

Kayak — the only "land anywhere" pass

Kayaks may land where boats can't (with summer exceptions at En-Vau/Port-Pin: moor to the cable, don't beach). Rentals are licence-controlled; guided trips dodge all of it. The most freedom per euro in the park.

Kayak & SUP trips →

Hike — earn the beach

The only way to actually lie on En-Vau's pebbles. Real grades, polished rock, zero water — the park's own time estimates run about double what fit-hiker blogs quote, and both are honest. Guided hikes (~€40) solve navigation and water anxiety.

Guided hikes →

The wind rule nobody explains: the mistral (NW) and the E/SE swell cancel different things. Cassis and Sormiou sit in the mistral's lee — their boats cancel on easterly swell instead. Marseille's crossings and Frioul feel the mistral head-on. And the calmest month of the year, per Météo-France's own normals, is August — boat plans are safest exactly when trail plans are shakiest. Closures & reservations — the three-layer system

Three independent mechanisms decide access, and travel content routinely mashes them into one wrong rule. The five-minute version:

Layer 1 · Jun–Sep

The nightly fire map

Each massif is declared open or red for tomorrow, published ~17:00–18:00 tonight. Red = no land access at all, even by sea. Boats still sail; Frioul stays open. Rare on the coast: 3 red days so far in 2026.

Layer 2 · calendar

Road closures (cars only)

The Sormiou, Morgiou and Callelongue roads shut to cars daily 12 Jun–30 Aug, 07:00–19:00 — and in September, weekends close Sormiou and Morgiou only. Trails stay open; this layer just deletes the parking.

Layer 3 · one calanque

The Sugiton permit

Free, nominative, compulsory in summer windows. Opens D-3 at 09:00, closes D-1 at 18:00. Photo ID for everyone; download the permit before leaving — there's no signal at the cove. €68 without one.

Full detail — the exact URL to check at 18:05, the 2026 calendars, no-show tricks for sold-out Sugiton dates, and every fine amount — in the closures & reservations guide.

Season by season

Oct–Apr — the locals' park

Every trail open, no permits, no road gates, climbing season at its peak. Sea 13–16°C (wetsuit territory), and the windiest months of the year are Feb–Apr — check the wind rules before booking a boat.

May–mid-June — the sweet spot

Warm sea by June, wildflowers (the endemic Sabline de Provence blooms Apr–Jun), full access until the closure regime starts 1 June, crowds still civilised. Book sailing days before the school holidays do.

Late June–Aug — system season

The nightly map, road gates, the Sugiton permit, 30°C+ on shadeless rock. Also: the calmest sea of the year and every boat and catamaran running daily. Play it sea-first, trail-at-dawn.

Sept — the insiders' summer

Sea at its warmest, permits down to two September weekends, roads reopen weekdays — only Sormiou and Morgiou keep weekend gates. The Marseille–Cassis race (25 Oct 2026) blacks out the Gorguettes shuttle for a day.

Beyond the basics

Four things worth knowing exist even though we haven't given them full pages yet:

Climbing

World-class limestone with a twist: the nesting-bird closures run winter–spring and the fire closures run summer — crags are open when the massif is closed and vice versa. Nothing here is a real via ferrata; it's all guided via cordata. Small-operator trips appear in the catalogue's climbing note.

Diving

Boat dives are the park's most closure-proof activity (they never land). Two archaeological no-dive zones exist — including Cousteau's own Grand Congloué site. The famous wrecks sit off Île de Planier, not Riou. See dive & snorkel trips.

Cycling

Bikes are legal only on the park's mapped network (you may not even push a bike elsewhere), e-bikes over 250 W count as motor vehicles, and Frioul bans bikes outright. The park's English page still shows the repealed pre-2021 rules. Guided e-bike tours stay inside the legal network for you.

Your own boat

Legal and lovely — with a licensing system: rent only from operators showing the park's green vignette (the park explicitly warns the customer is liable). No-licence boats (6 hp cap) realistically work from Cassis only. Anchoring over Posidonia is the fast lane to a fine — details in the boat guide.

The mistakes everyone makes

Trusting the park's English pages

The FR site is authoritative; the EN pages are stale in at least five places (wrong Sugiton dates, repealed bike rules, retired bus lines). We cite French sources and translate.

Planning "a boat drop-off at En-Vau"

Banned since the 2000s, banned everywhere else too. Any itinerary that includes "get dropped at the beach" was written from a 1990s memory.

Packing one small bottle of water

Zero water points inside the park, ~zero shade in the coves, polished rock underfoot. 1.5 L per half-day minimum, double in heat. Open shoes are formally advised against.

Reading "orange" as "open-ish"

The access decision is binary: open or red. Sites explaining orange levels are quoting a rule abrogated in 2025. Check tonight's map, not a blog.

Assuming lifeguards

Two beaches have them in summer: Sormiou and Saint-Estève. The famous six-beach list circulating online is water-quality monitoring, not surveillance. En-Vau has neither.

Booking swim cruises with young kids

Marseille's swim-stop cruises refuse under-10s (one also refuses pregnant women); swims are open-water from the boat. Take the family to a lifeguarded beach instead.

Flying the drone

All motorised overflight under 1,000 m is banned over the core. This is one of the most-fined visitor mistakes, and rangers do patrol with e-ticket books (340 fines in 2024).

Bringing cash-only assumptions to Cassis

Cassis boats: buy same-day at the kiosk, avoid the 14:30–16:00 crush. Marseille's biggest operator opens sales only 24–48 h ahead and not online — while resellers happily sell months out.

When a tour genuinely beats DIY

Most of this park is free and self-serve, and this site tells you when to keep your money. A booked tour wins in exactly three situations:

  • You want the water, not the logistics. A catamaran or sailing day turns the no-landing rule into a feature: swim stops, lunch aboard, zero transit puzzles.
  • You want a beach the hard way, safely. Guided hikes (~€40) and guided kayak trips handle navigation, water discipline and the rules you'd otherwise learn by fine.
  • Your time is fixed and the system isn't. Cruise stopovers and one-day visits can't absorb a red day — e-bike loops and boat circuits reschedule around closures far better than a solo trail plan.
