Getting to the Calanques: the bus beats your rental car

There is no transport that reaches a calanque — every bus, train and ferry delivers you to a trailhead or a village at the edge, and then you walk. That is not the bad news. The bad news is that 92% of visitors still drive, into a corridor with roughly 250 parking spaces for 2,600 cars, past road gates that are locked all summer. Here is the network that actually works, with the fares actually in force.

TL;DR

  • Marseille side: a €5.20 24-hour pass covers the metro plus bus B1 to Luminy (for Sugiton), bus 22 to Les Baumettes (Morgiou), bus 23 to La Cayolle (Sormiou) and buses 19 + 20 to Callelongue. No tour, taxi or hire car improves on it.
  • Cassis side: TER train, then the €0.90 Gorguettes shuttle system — bus 373 drops you at the Presqu'île, minutes from the Port-Miou trailhead. Drivers: the free Gorguettes park-and-ride is the only sane plan in summer.
  • Fares people get wrong: the RTM single is €1.70 (€2.00 bought on board) under the tariff in force since September 2025 — the €1.50 and €1.90 figures all over the web are stale. The Frioul ferry jumped ~60% to €17.70/€26.00 in July 2026.
  • Do not drive to Sormiou, Morgiou or Callelongue between 12 June and 30 August: the roads are gated 07:00–19:00 daily and roadside parking costs €135 plus the pound. Details on the closures page.

The Marseille gateways — four buses and a boat

The backbone is simple: metro M1 or M2 to Castellane or Rond-Point du Prado, then one (or two) surface buses to the park edge. Board by the front door, validate immediately — contactless bank cards now work directly on the validators — and expect to stand in summer. Verified: 30 July 2026

Two stale claims to ignore. Older guides send you to Sugiton on “bus 21” — the park's own page names B1 as the line (it also lists a 521 for evenings, but we could not verify its year-round pattern — check RTM before relying on it). And no shuttle serves Sormiou, despite “22S shuttle” claims circulating on aggregator sites: bus 23 to La Cayolle or bus 22 to Les Baumettes is as close as public transport gets.

Bus 20: the workhorse that got serious in 2026

The Madrague ⇆ Callelongue leg runs every 20 minutes from 06:30 to 23:00 daily (every 15 minutes since 29 May), year-round including holidays, dropping to every 30 minutes on summer evenings. The three 22-seat minibuses were replaced by 42-seat buses in 2025–26, doubling capacity, and the 19 and 20 timetables are now synchronised at Montredon so the connection actually works. It needs to: during the 2026 heatwave bus 19 carried about 14,000 trips a day (+10% on 2025) and bus 20 was up about 25%. Expect to stand, and expect to be left behind at Madrague if the 20 fills.

The hour matters more than the line. The pressure window is 10:00–16:00. Buses 19 and 20 start at 06:30 while the first Cassis shuttles only run from 09:00 — so on the Marseille side, an early start doesn't just beat the queue, it beats the entire parking problem. Transport's genuinely quiet season is mid-September to late October and April to mid-June on weekdays: the seasonal shuttles are still running, the road gates are mostly open, and nobody is fighting for a seat.

The sea shuttle: arriving by water for €8

RTM's lanavette is the gentlest approach to the park's edge in existence — a public commuter boat, not a tour. The 2026 mechanics:

  • Pointe-Rouge ⇆ Les Goudes: daily 30 May–30 August 2026, hourly — from Pointe-Rouge 08:50–19:50, from Les Goudes 09:20–20:20 — on a 48-seat boat. RTM's own pages disagree on whether the crossing takes 8 or 15 minutes, so call it “short”.
  • Vieux-Port ⇆ Pointe-Rouge: the connecting line (123 seats, 20–25 min) runs 25 April–27 September, hourly, with three extra late sailings 27 June–30 August. The Les Goudes boat calls at Pointe-Rouge, so you can ride through to the Vieux-Port on one ticket.
  • Fares: €5 single per line, or €8 Vieux-Port to Les Goudes with a guaranteed connection and 3 h of network interchange. Sold on board only — and the 24h/72h passes and CityPass do not cover it.
  • Two hard rules: a valid ticket does not guarantee boarding once the 48 seats are gone (be at the quay early), and sailings cancel in strong mistral — this is the most weather-exposed link in the network.

Bikes and scooters: now a real option

LeVélo bike-share stations opened in spring 2025 at Mont-Rose, Saména, L'Escalette, Les Goudes and Callelongue (~50 bikes each), Lime and Voi scooters cover the corridor as far as Callelongue, and the coastal road is limited to 30 km/h with racks along it. Inside the park core, bikes are restricted to a mapped positive-list network and e-bikes above 250 W count as motor vehicles — so ride to the gateway, then walk. If you'd rather have the logistics handled, the guided e-bike tours run the same coast with a licence to make it easy.

Arriving on a cruise ship?

The cruise terminal sits on the far north side of Marseille — the wrong side of the city from every gateway above, with two metro-plus-bus legs and a hard sail-away deadline between you and the park. If your shore day cannot absorb transit risk, the half-day e-bike tour that starts at the cruise port itself (★4.7, 288 reviews) is the one format built for exactly this problem: it leaves from where you are, reaches the calanques viewpoints under its own power, and is timed around ship departures rather than bus timetables.

The Cassis side — train, shuttle, and one clever coach

Cassis is the gateway for Port-Miou, Port-Pin and En-Vau. The detail almost every guide buries: Cassis station is not in Cassis. It sits at 128 m altitude, about 3 km from the port on the Roquefort-la-Bédoule road — not a pleasant walk in either direction and definitely not with beach kit in August. From the station, lebus M1 “la Marcouline” runs to the Casino in the centre in about 20 minutes (€0.80–€0.90, 7 days a week all year except 1 January, roughly 06:40–20:45) — but it is a timetabled line with about 13 runs a day each way, not a turn-up-and-go shuttle, so check the sheet before you rely on it.

The Gorguettes system: M1, 372, 373 — €0.90 a ride

At the entrance to Cassis sits the free Gorguettes park-and-ride (roughly 220–230 spaces plus 8 disabled bays, municipal police present, tourist-office desk staffed daily in July–August). Note what the 373's dates mean: outside 4 April–1 November there is no bus to the Presqu'île — you walk from the port (which is pleasant, and only adds ~20 minutes). And the 372 has exactly one blackout: the last Sunday of October, race day (see disruptions).

lecar 78 from Marseille — and the Lachamp Luminy trick

The direct coach is lecar line 78, Castellane (avenue de Toulon) to Cassis via La Gineste: about 35 minutes at quiet times, 40–45 at peak, roughly 19 weekday runs, hourly on Saturdays, extending from La Ciotat in high season. The trick nobody prints: the 78 stops at “Lachamp Luminy”, so it links the Cassis side to the Luminy/Sugiton gateway without ever entering central Marseille — useful for one-way traverse days, since the park's own three-stage traverse is explicitly designed around the Gorguettes shuttle at one end and the B1 terminus at the other.

Three caveats on the 78:

  • The exact fare product for the full Marseille–Cassis run wasn't confirmable from the operator's zone map — budget the €3.50 all-zones ticket and treat anything less as a bonus.
  • Older sources call this coach “M6” or “M8”. Both numbers are dead — the M8 timetable still hosted on Cassis tourist-office servers is stamped September 2021. Live M-numbers are the Marcouline town lines only (M1, M5).
  • The operator posted a disruption notice for lines 68/78 at Cassis effective 6 July 2026 — check lecaraixmarseille.com (plain text on purpose) before building a day around it.

La Ciotat — renumbered buses and the calanque with no bus at all

The whole Ciotabus network was renumbered on 7 July 2025 — old lines 10, 20, 21, 30, 31 and 52 became 351–357 — and the park's own La Ciotat access page still lists the retired numbers. Any guide (official or not) telling you to take “line 30 to Mugel and Figuerolles” is quoting a network that no longer exists. A further tweak on 16 February 2026 altered lines 356/357 and reorganised the Office de Tourisme hub. Verified: 30 July 2026

What actually matters for the Mugel and Figuerolles calanques, on a €0.90 flat fare paid to the driver:

  • Line 355 (Le Mugel ⇆ Athélia 1) terminates at Calanque du Mugel — every 22–30 min on weekdays in term time, 30–60 min Saturdays. It does not run on Sundays, which is exactly when day-trippers arrive. Lines 354 and 356 skip Sundays too; 351, 352, 353 and 357 do run.
  • Figuerolles: line 353 stops at “Figuerolles” (~200 m from the cove) and, unlike the 355, runs on Sundays — or just walk ~20 minutes from the Office de Tourisme.
  • Regional links: lecar 69 to Aubagne, 79 to Marseille, and the 78 to Cassis/Marseille — but the La Ciotat leg of the 78 is seasonal, April to October only. Île Verte is reached by boat from the Vieux-Port de La Ciotat, April–October.

The practical read: a Sunday calanques day at La Ciotat is a walking day from the town centre — plan it as such, or aim for a Saturday. If you're coming for the water rather than the coves, the kayak & SUP page explains why La Ciotat is the least-regulated paddling base in the park.

The Frioul ferry — new fares, same 35 minutes

The Frioul archipelago and Château d'If are served by one public ferry, lebateau Frioul If, from the Gare Maritime at 1 quai de la Fraternité — the head of the Vieux-Port, 10–15 minutes from Saint-Charles station on metro M1. It runs 7 days a week, all year, from about 06:30, on 160-seat vessels: about 20 minutes to If, about 35 minutes to the Port du Frioul. Daytime sailings call at If then continue; evening sailings skip If entirely.

The 2026 fares nobody's guidebook has caught up with

New fares took force on 1 July 2026: one-island return €17.70, two islands (If + Frioul) €26.00, single €9.60. That is a rise of roughly 60% on 2025's €11.10 — any source quoting €10.80–€11.10 is out of date. Reduced family, group and Métropole-resident fares exist; under-4s ride free. Verified: 30 July 2026

The consolation prize is real: an €5.60 evening return to Frioul for departures from 19:00, 1 July–30 September — counter-only, same day, sold after 18:10, no online sale, and the boats run back until close to midnight. It's the cheapest sea time Marseille sells, and sunset from Pomègues is the best free thing the archipelago offers. Two access notes worth knowing before you book anything: the If landing stage is not wheelchair-accessible (the boats and the Frioul pier are), and on red fire-risk days Frioul is not a rated massif — it stays legally open when every mainland trail is shut, which makes this ferry the park's red-day loophole.

The driving truth — 2,600 cars, 250 spaces, one pound

The park's own access plan states that 92% of arrivals at its five gateways are by private car. Here is what those drivers meet in summer 2026: Verified: 30 July 2026

Gated all summer

The three calanque roads

Sormiou, Morgiou and Callelongue roads: closed to unauthorised vehicles 07:00–19:00 on 5–7 June and every day from 12 June to 30 August 2026, plus September weekends for Sormiou and Morgiou only. The arrêté is explicit that the gates operate regardless of the daily fire level — it's a saturation measure, and it predates the park itself (the closures date to 1959). On red fire days the gates run 24/7. Permits go only to cabanon holders, boat owners and their contractors — never to visitors. One gap we must flag: marseille.fr was serving a maintenance page when we tried to re-verify the per-gate hour details beyond the shared 07:00–19:00 window, so treat any finer-grained hours you find elsewhere as unconfirmed.

Enforced daily

The parking mathematics

Roughly 2,600 cars tried to reach Les Goudes daily in summer 2026, for about 250 legal spaces (La Provence's count). Roadside parking on the corridor is a €135 fixed fine plus possible impound — municipal police attend daily, video cameras and a speed radar watch the route des Goudes, and the pound operates on site. This is not a theoretical risk; it is the business model of your afternoon going wrong. Full closure calendars here.

If you must bring the car

Use it to reach a park-and-ride, then stop being a driver. The clean play is the Gorguettes at Cassis: free parking, €0.90 shuttle to town or the Presqu'île trailhead. On the Marseille side, park at any RTM park-and-ride on the metro network and ride to Castellane or Rond-Point du Prado for the gateway buses. Paid town car parks in Cassis exist (Mimosas and Viguerie ~450 m from the port, La Poste 300 m) but fill early in season. And for what it's worth: no tour beats the public network on access — the honest case for a boat tour is seeing calanques you'd otherwise walk 4 hours for, or salvaging a red day, not transport. On a day when everything cooperates, the €5.20 day pass is the best travel product in the region.

Add a 60-minute buffer to any summer afternoon plan for a full bus at Madrague or a full boat at Les Goudes — both genuinely happen, and the last-departure risk is the thing that turns a moderate day hard. Bus 20 runs to 23:00, but the Cassis 373 stops at 20:00 and Ciotabus 355 doesn't run Sundays at all: rate any calanque one grade harder if you're relying on the day's last departure.

Rules of the ride (the ones that catch visitors)

  • Board RTM buses by the front door and validate immediately; alight at the rear. No ticket = €100.
  • Pushchairs must be folded on board; bikes, rollers and skateboards are not allowed inside RTM or Ciotabus vehicles.
  • Animals are generally banned on the RTM network (small animals in carriers and guide dogs excepted) — awkwardly, since dogs on a lead are allowed in the park itself. Plan around it.
  • RTM travel is free for under-11s; the 65+ concession applies to metropolitan residents only — visiting seniors pay, but the €5.20/€10.80 passes are cheap.
  • Carry small cash anyway: Ciotabus and the Gorguettes shuttles are paid at the driver, and Nautic Bar at Morgiou is cash-only.

Matching mode to traveller

  • With small children: the €8 Vieux-Port → Les Goudes sea shuttle is a better day than any land approach — the boat is the attraction and there's no walking beyond the quay. Callelongue via 19+20 and the Cassis Presqu'île via 373 are the realistic bus options; strollers die at the paved edges everywhere.
  • Mobility-limited: the sea shuttle again, or buses 19, 20 and B1, which have wheelchair-accessible stops marked on RTM's route plans — the trails beyond are not accessible, but Les Goudes, Callelongue's port and the Frioul village (accessible pier) are worthwhile destinations in themselves.
  • Flying in: the airport shuttle to Saint-Charles is €8.30 single (€9.20 with RTM network access included), every 15 minutes, 25 minutes — you can be at a trailhead about 90 minutes after leaving arrivals.
  • Carrying your day on your back: there are no water points, toilets, shops or bins anywhere on the trails or beaches — the park's floor is 1.5 litres per person per half-day, more in heat. Load up before the last bus stop, not after.

When paying someone actually beats the bus

Being honest about our own subject: for pure access, DIY wins almost every match-up. A €5.20 day pass reaches trailheads no coach can, and no commercial shuttle-plus-hike product beats the public network on cost or coverage. The exceptions are specific:

  • One day, no car, and you want En-Vau: the walk costs you 4 hours round trip plus 3 hours of transport. A Cassis boat circuit shows you En-Vau and Port-Pin in 90 minutes — you see them, you don't land, and you should know that before booking.
  • A red fire day: the boats still sail and the trails don't open for anyone. This is the one scenario where the tour is objectively the better product, not a compromise.
  • A cruise-ship clock: covered above — a tour that starts at the terminal removes the transit-timing risk entirely.
  • Everything else — including guided hikes, which ride the same buses and queue in the same Sugiton quota as you — is a service purchase (navigation, safety, logistics), not an access purchase. See the guided hikes with that framing in mind.
